# DSP-Lab Student Experience Questionnaire

## Purpose

This short questionnaire is designed to measure students' perceptions of DSP-Lab as a mobile-assisted after-class self-learning tool. It is intentionally lightweight so that it can be administered immediately after the post-test.

This is a researcher-developed questionnaire. It should be pilot-tested before use in the main study and should not be described as a previously validated scale.

## Instructions to students

Please indicate how strongly you agree or disagree with each statement based on your experience using DSP-Lab.

1 = Strongly disagree
2 = Disagree
3 = Neutral
4 = Agree
5 = Strongly agree

## Items

| No. | Statement | Suggested construct |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | DSP-Lab helped me understand difficult DSP concepts more clearly. | Perceived learning usefulness |
| 2 | The interactive plots helped me connect mathematical concepts with signal behavior. | Visualization usefulness |
| 3 | Changing parameters and immediately seeing the result helped me learn DSP. | Interactivity usefulness |
| 4 | The five learning activities were relevant to the DSP topics taught in class. | Content relevance |
| 5 | DSP-Lab was easy to operate without extensive instructions. | Ease of use |
| 6 | Using DSP-Lab on a mobile device was convenient for after-class study. | Mobile convenience |
| 7 | The investigation questions encouraged me to think before accepting the displayed result. | Active learning |
| 8 | Writing a short reflection helped me explain what I had observed. | Reflective learning |
| 9 | DSP-Lab supported my ability to study DSP independently outside class. | Self-directed learning |
| 10 | I would be willing to use similar mobile interactive activities for other DSP topics. | Behavioral intention |

## Optional open-ended questions

1. Which DSP-Lab activity helped you most, and why?
2. What was the main difficulty or inconvenience you experienced while using DSP-Lab?
3. What is one change that would improve DSP-Lab for after-class learning?

## Scoring

The simplest approach is to report:

- item-level mean and standard deviation;
- overall questionnaire mean across Items 1–10;
- internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha) for the 10-item set, with appropriate caution because the questionnaire contains several related but distinct constructs.

If the final sample is sufficiently large, exploratory factor analysis may be considered, but it is not required for the initial teaching study.

## Recommended reporting

Avoid treating a high satisfaction score as proof of learning effectiveness. Questionnaire findings should be reported as a secondary outcome alongside the objective pre/post assessment.

Example manuscript wording:

> Students in the intervention group completed a 10-item, five-point Likert questionnaire assessing perceived usefulness, visualization and interaction support, ease of use, mobile convenience, reflective learning, self-directed learning, and intention to reuse the tool. The questionnaire was researcher-developed and pilot-reviewed before the main study.
