# Expert Review Method Note — Content Validity

## Purpose

This note documents the methodological basis for summarizing expert ratings of the DSP-Lab Form A/Form B concept-test items. It is intended for the research team and for later use when writing the Methods section.

## Verified methodological source

Polit, D. F., Beck, C. T., & Owen, S. V. (2007). *Is the CVI an acceptable indicator of content validity? Appraisal and recommendations*. **Research in Nursing & Health, 30**(4), 459–467. https://doi.org/10.1002/nur.20199

The article is indexed in PubMed (PMID 17654487) and published by Wiley. It discusses the item-level content validity index (I-CVI), scale-level summaries, and chance agreement. The authors indicate that an I-CVI of approximately 0.78 or higher can be considered evidence of good content validity when there are three or more experts.

## How this applies to the DSP-Lab study

The DSP-Lab expert panel is intentionally small (target: 2–3 experienced DSP instructors) because the review is a focused content check before a student pilot rather than a stand-alone scale-development study.

For each Form A and Form B item, reviewers rate **relevance** on a 4-point scale. Ratings of 3 or 4 are treated as endorsements.

- **I-CVI** = number of experts rating relevance 3 or 4 / number of experts who rated the item.
- **S-CVI/Ave** = average of the 24 item-level relevance I-CVI values across the 12 Form A and 12 Form B items.

With exactly three experts, the possible endorsement proportions are 0, 0.333, 0.667, and 1.000. Therefore, applying a 0.78 screening criterion effectively requires unanimous endorsement from all three experts for an item.

Clarity, distractor quality, and Form A/Form B equivalence are also important, but they should not be mislabeled as relevance CVIs. They are summarized separately using mean ratings and endorsement proportions.

## Publication-oriented interpretation

The manuscript should avoid claiming that the concept test is “validated” solely because the CVI is high. A stronger and more accurate sequence is:

1. expert content review;
2. revision based on quantitative ratings and written comments;
3. student pilot examining difficulty, discrimination, internal consistency, and approximate Form A/Form B comparability;
4. final instrument freeze before the main quasi-experimental study.

A suitable Methods statement after the review and pilot is:

> Content relevance and parallel-form equivalence were reviewed by experienced DSP instructors before pilot testing. Item relevance was summarized using item-level content validity indices, while clarity, distractor quality, and A/B equivalence were examined separately. The revised forms were then piloted with students to examine item difficulty, discrimination, internal consistency, and approximate form comparability.

## Reporting cautions

- Report the number and relevant teaching experience of reviewers.
- State the exact rating scale and endorsement rule.
- Preserve written comments and a revision log.
- Do not delete items mechanically from a small expert panel; use ratings together with content coverage and reviewer comments.
- Do not present a high CVI as evidence of criterion validity, construct validity, or learning-effect validity.
