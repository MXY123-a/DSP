# DSP-Lab Consent and Ethics Template

> This document is a research-planning template, not legal or institutional-ethics advice. Adapt it to the requirements of the university, ethics committee/IRB, course context, and applicable law before use.

## 1. Key ethical principles for this study

Because participants are students enrolled in a course, the protocol should minimize perceived pressure to participate. In particular:

- research participation should be voluntary where required by the approved protocol;
- choosing not to participate should not reduce grades, access to instruction, or teacher support;
- if the instructor is also the researcher, consider having consent and research-file collection handled by another staff member where feasible;
- research data should use anonymous or pseudonymous participant codes rather than names or student numbers;
- the participant-code key, if one is needed, should be stored separately with restricted access;
- only data necessary for the stated research questions should be collected;
- publication should use aggregate statistics and de-identified excerpts only.

## 2. Suggested participant information text

### Study title

**Mobile-Assisted After-Class Learning in Undergraduate Digital Signal Processing**

### Purpose

You are invited to take part in a teaching-and-learning study examining whether a mobile interactive DSP learning tool can support undergraduate students' understanding of Digital Signal Processing concepts during after-class study.

### What participation involves

Depending on your assigned learning condition, you will complete regular course learning plus either mobile interactive learning activities or conventional after-class review activities. The research may include a pre-test, post-test, short learning activities, a brief questionnaire, and analysis of anonymous learning records.

### Time commitment

The five learning activities are designed as short after-class activities, generally around 15–30 minutes each. The pre-test, post-test, and questionnaire require additional brief periods.

### Data collected

The research dataset may include:

- anonymous participant code;
- pre-test and post-test scores;
- activity-completion status;
- time on task for the mobile-learning group;
- submitted reflection text;
- questionnaire responses;
- reflection rubric scores generated during research analysis.

Do not enter your name, student number, email address, or phone number into the DSP-Lab research export.

### Risks and inconvenience

This is a minimal-risk educational study. Possible inconvenience includes the time required to complete the learning activities, tests, and questionnaire. Some students may feel mild frustration when answering unfamiliar DSP questions. Participation does not require disclosure of sensitive personal information in the research dataset.

### Potential benefits

You may benefit from additional opportunities to review DSP concepts and visualize the effects of changing signal-processing parameters. Individual learning improvement cannot be guaranteed.

### Voluntary participation

Participation in the research is voluntary, subject to the procedures approved by the institution. A decision not to participate, or to withdraw where permitted, will not affect your course grade, access to teaching materials, or relationship with the instructor or university.

### Confidentiality

Research files will use an anonymous participant code. Any code key linking participants to codes, if required, will be stored separately from the analysis dataset. Results will be reported in aggregate. If anonymous reflection excerpts are quoted, identifying details will be removed.

## 3. Suggested consent statement

Use only after adapting this text to the institution's approved wording:

> I have read the study information, had the opportunity to ask questions, and understand what participation involves. I understand that research participation is voluntary and that my decision will not affect my course grade or access to instruction. I consent to the use of my de-identified study data for the research purposes described above.

Possible response options:

- I consent to participate in the research.
- I do not consent to participate in the research.

Do not treat silence or failure to respond as consent unless the approved institutional procedure explicitly allows that approach.

## 4. Course activity versus research participation

If DSP-Lab is used as part of normal course teaching for all students, separate **participation in the learning activity** from **permission to use the resulting data for research**. The ethics protocol should specify how data from students who decline research participation will be excluded from the research dataset without disadvantaging them academically.

## 5. Special considerations when the teacher is the researcher

Recommended safeguards include:

- avoid viewing identifiable participation decisions before grades are finalized, where practical;
- use anonymous codes for exported files;
- avoid offering grade bonuses that could be perceived as coercive unless an equivalent non-research alternative is provided and approved;
- make clear that correct test answers and positive questionnaire responses are not required;
- do not evaluate reflection quality for course grading if the same reflections are being used as research data unless that dual use is clearly disclosed and approved.

## 6. Publication and qualitative excerpts

If reflection excerpts are used in a paper:

- quote only de-identified text;
- remove accidental personal references;
- select excerpts to illustrate the coding results, not only favorable opinions;
- follow the institution's approved consent language for quotation of student responses.

## 7. Pre-study ethics checklist

- Determine whether ethics/IRB review, exemption, or institutional teaching-research approval is required.
- Obtain approval before recruiting participants or collecting research data when required.
- Finalize the participant information sheet and consent process.
- Define whether minors could be enrolled and, if so, follow the required guardian/assent procedure.
- Define withdrawal procedures and the point after which fully anonymized data can no longer be linked back for removal.
- Define data retention duration and access controls.
- Keep the participant-code key separate from the research dataset.
- Do not collect direct identifiers in the APP export.
- Document the approved protocol number and approval date in the manuscript.
