# DSP-Lab Main-Study Fidelity Protocol

This protocol is used during the five-week main teaching study to document whether the experimental and control conditions were delivered as planned. The aim is to support a defensible interpretation of the group comparison without expanding the study beyond the prespecified publication scope.

## 1. Core principle

Both groups receive the same regular DSP classroom instruction. The planned difference is the after-class learning activity:

- Experimental group: structured DSP-Lab mobile activity with prediction, parameter manipulation, immediate visualization, comparison, and reflection.
- Control group: structured conventional review covering the same topic with worked examples and written comparison/explanation.

The study should avoid introducing other systematic differences between groups.

## 2. Five-week delivery schedule

| Week | DSP topic | Experimental activity | Control activity | Intended time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Discrete signals | DSP-Lab Lab 1 | Matched conventional signals review | 15–30 min |
| 2 | Sampling and aliasing | DSP-Lab Lab 2 | Matched conventional sampling review | 15–30 min |
| 3 | Convolution | DSP-Lab Lab 3 | Matched conventional convolution review | 15–30 min |
| 4 | DFT and spectrum | DSP-Lab Lab 4 | Matched conventional DFT review | 15–30 min |
| 5 | FIR filtering | DSP-Lab Lab 5 | Matched conventional FIR review | 15–30 min |

## 3. What must be held comparable

For each week, keep the following as similar as practical across groups:

- topic and learning objective;
- date or release window;
- completion deadline;
- number and timing of reminders;
- normal classroom teaching received before the after-class activity;
- access to normal instructor support;
- intended after-class study duration.

Do not intentionally provide one group with additional worked solutions, extra tutoring, extra graded credit, or extra assessment preparation that the other group does not receive.

## 4. Experimental-group fidelity

Each week confirm that:

- the frozen DSP-Lab build was used;
- the planned lab was available and functioned sufficiently for use;
- students were instructed to complete the corresponding structured activity;
- no assessment answer key or Form A/Form B item solution was embedded into the weekly activity;
- major technical problems were recorded rather than silently corrected with a materially different version during the study.

App elapsed duration is treated as a proxy for exposure, not a validated measure of continuous cognitive engagement.

## 5. Control-group fidelity

Each week confirm that:

- the planned matched review activity was used;
- the content objective matched the experimental week;
- the intended workload remained approximately comparable;
- the control group did not use DSP-Lab as part of the assigned intervention before post-test completion;
- additional interactive software was not systematically introduced as a substitute for the conventional review condition.

Control-group study time may be self-reported or recorded using the planned course procedure, but it should not be treated as directly equivalent to app-recorded elapsed time.

## 6. Reminder policy

Use the same reminder rule for both groups. Record the number of reminders actually sent each week. If a reminder differs by group, record the reason.

The reminder procedure should support completion without becoming a second intervention.

## 7. Instructor behavior

The instructor may answer ordinary course-content questions for both groups. However, avoid condition-specific coaching that effectively adds an additional instructional treatment.

If unusual extra help is provided to one group, record it as an instructor deviation.

## 8. Contamination and crossover

Record any credible evidence that:

- control students accessed or used DSP-Lab before the post-test;
- experimental students relied primarily on the control worksheet instead of the assigned DSP-Lab activity;
- students exchanged intervention materials across conditions;
- one condition received the other condition's material through a teaching or technical error.

Do not automatically exclude affected students unless the prespecified analysis rules require it. Use contamination information primarily for transparent reporting and, if justified before outcome inspection, limited sensitivity analysis.

## 9. Weekly deviation categories

A fidelity deviation includes any meaningful departure from the frozen protocol, including:

- wrong activity or topic;
- different deadline or release period between groups;
- materially different reminder exposure;
- major app failure;
- missing control material;
- unusual class cancellation or disruption;
- instructor giving one group substantial extra help;
- contamination/crossover;
- assessment administered outside the planned sequence;
- mid-study intervention material change.

Minor formatting or administrative differences that cannot plausibly influence learning may be noted without being treated as major deviations.

## 10. Weekly record

For each group and week record at minimum:

- date;
- topic;
- planned activity;
- whether delivered as planned;
- release window and deadline;
- reminder count;
- number assigned;
- number completed;
- typical or median study time if available;
- technical issue;
- contamination observed;
- instructor deviation;
- schedule deviation;
- concise notes;
- person recording the entry.

Use aggregate counts in the fidelity record. Do not place student names or direct identifiers in this log.

## 11. Participant flow

At the end of the study summarize, by group:

- assigned/allocated;
- pre-test completed;
- Week 1 completed;
- Week 2 completed;
- Week 3 completed;
- Week 4 completed;
- Week 5 completed;
- post-test completed;
- included in the primary analysis.

Reasons for missing post-tests or exclusion from the primary dataset should be reported transparently.

## 12. Analysis relationship

The fidelity record does not replace the prespecified primary analysis and should not be used to select a favorable subgroup after seeing results.

The primary analysis remains based on valid group assignment plus valid pre/post scores according to the analysis lock. Adherence/completion information is descriptive unless a sensitivity analysis was justified and specified before examining group outcomes.

## 13. Important design limitation

If the study uses only one intact class per condition, class effects and intervention effects cannot be fully separated statistically. Fidelity documentation improves transparency but does not remove this confounding. The limitation should be acknowledged directly in the manuscript.

## 14. Manuscript reporting sentence

A concise Methods statement can be adapted after the study:

> Intervention delivery was monitored weekly using a prespecified fidelity record. Both groups received the same regular classroom instruction and completed topic-matched after-class activities over five weeks with comparable release windows, deadlines, and reminder procedures; implementation deviations and potential cross-condition contamination were documented prospectively.

Do not insert completion percentages or fidelity claims into the manuscript until the actual study records are available.
